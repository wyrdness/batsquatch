## Overview

Batsquatch is a modern American cryptid associated with the volcanic and forested terrain around Mount St. Helens in Washington State. The name is a portmanteau of 'bat' and 'Sasquatch'. The creature is described as roughly nine feet tall with a humanoid, ape-like body, leathery bat-like wings, blue or purple fur, sharp teeth, yellow eyes, and a wolf- or dog-like muzzle. The most cited encounter is the April 1994 report by 18-year-old Brian Canfield, who said his pickup truck stalled on a remote road south of Mount Rainier and a winged figure landed in front of him before flying away; the Tacoma News Tribune ran the story and reporter C. R. Roberts is credited with coining the name 'Batsquatch'. Older popular accounts also tie the cryptid to the period following the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens, but contemporary 1980 reports are not well documented in mainstream sources.

## Notable Sightings

### Brian Canfield encounter ⭐
> **Date**: 1994-04-16  
> **Location**: Remote forest road south of Mount Rainier, Pierce County  
> **Witnesses**: 1 (Local resident (18-year-old Brian Canfield))  
Driving home alone at night through wooded country in Pierce County, Brian Canfield's pickup truck reportedly stalled on a remote road. He described a roughly nine-foot tall winged creature with blue fur, a wolf-like muzzle, sharp teeth, yellow eyes, and large leathery wings landing in front of his vehicle. After several minutes the creature took off and flew away; the truck then started normally. Canfield drove home and reported the incident; the story was carried by the Tacoma News Tribune, where reporter C. R. Roberts coined the name 'Batsquatch'.
**Credibility**: low — The defining and most widely reported Batsquatch encounter.
**Sources**: Tacoma News Tribune (C. R. Roberts), April 1994; Cryptid encyclopedias (Coleman & Clark, others)

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Volcanic / interdimensional cryptid** _(Cryptid bloggers and authors)_

Batsquatch belongs to the broader winged-humanoid cryptid type associated with major geological or industrial sites; some cryptid writers tie its emergence to the energy of the Mount St. Helens eruption.

### Skeptical

**Misidentification** _(Skeptical writers on Pacific Northwest cryptids)_

The Canfield report and similar accounts may reflect misidentification of large birds (great horned owls, golden eagles) at night, possibly compounded by fatigue and the influence of pop-culture cryptid imagery.

**Folkloric construct** _(Folklorists of contemporary legend)_

Batsquatch is best understood as a regional modern legend reinforced by media and tourism, not as a biological candidate.
